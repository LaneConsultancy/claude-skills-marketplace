Ranking factors, review benchmarks, citation tiers, and confirmed non-factors for local search.

## 2026 Ranking Weight Breakdown (Whitespark)

Local pack / Maps weighting:

| Signal category | Weight |
|-----------------|--------|
| GBP signals | 32% (largest controllable category) |
| Review signals | 20% (up from 16% in 2023) |
| On-page signals | 15-19% |
| Behavioural signals | ~9% (rising) |
| Link signals | ~8% |
| Citation signals | ~6% (rising, driven by AI) |
| Social signals | ~5% (back on the board) |

Proximity to the searcher is the single biggest factor overall (roughly **55%** of the decision) and is uncontrollable. Everything above is about maximising the controllable share.

## Confirmed High-Impact GBP Factors

1. **Primary category** -- the single most important individual factor. Wrong primary category is the #1 reason a business fails to rank. Be as specific as the list allows ("Heating contractor", not "Contractor"). Add secondary categories for every legitimate service line, but the primary carries most of the weight.
2. **Business hours / being open at time of search** -- fifth most important pack factor, new to prominence in 2026. Rankings measurably degrade in the final listed hour and while closed. Never leave a profile marked temporarily closed.
3. **Services section, using Google's predefined services** -- jumped from #81 to #22. Add every predefined service, then custom services with keyword-aligned descriptions. Feeds the structured-data parser and AI Overview answers.
4. **Review velocity** -- jumped from #93 to #11. A steady drip (~5/month, every month) beats a large stale total.
5. **Review responses** -- responding improves rankings; 80%+ response rate sees a measurable lift, amplified when replies land within 24-48h. Response text is indexed, so mention the service and town naturally.
6. **The website link (Diversity Update rule)** -- link the GBP to a strong service or location page, **not** a homepage that already ranks organically for money terms (Google may suppress a pack-ranking URL from page-one organic). UTM-tag it so GBP traffic is measurable.
7. **Photos** -- regular uploads of real work, team, and premises. Drive engagement and conversion (behavioural signals) and act as E-E-A-T evidence, not coordinates.
8. **NAP consistency** -- name, address, phone character-identical across website schema, GBP, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and core directories. Google cross-references these to verify the entity.

## Confirmed Non-Factors (stop spending time here)

- **GBP posts for ranking** -- Sterling Sky, 441 keywords, 9 weeks: zero movement. Engagement/CTR only.
- **The business description** -- Google confirmed it is not used in ranking. Write it for customers.
- **Service-area settings** (for a business with a listed address) -- Whitespark controlled test: no effect. You rank from your verified address.
- **Keywords in review text** -- Sterling Sky: no pack ranking impact. Still matter for AI surfacing and conversion.
- **Geotagged photos** -- Google strips EXIF on upload. Photos matter as content and E-E-A-T, not coordinates.

Note the flipside on names: keywords in the legal business name remain a strong signal, but adding keywords to a name you don't trade under violates guidelines and invites suspension. A niche name also creates an entity boundary that can suppress broader queries.

## Reviews as a System

- **Ask every satisfied customer immediately post-job** with a direct link (fewest taps). Automate it (e.g. ServiceM8 job-completion trigger).
- **Coach the ask** -- a review naming the service and town ("fixed our burst pipe in Dartford") outperforms five that say "Great!" AI systems read review text to decide query match. Don't script; prompt.
- **Velocity over volume** -- steady ~5/month forever beats a stale total.
- **Respond to everything within 24-48h**, negatives especially. Responses are indexed; mention service + town naturally.
- **Diversify** -- Checkatrade-type industry platforms and Facebook feed citation and AI-visibility signals, not just Google.

## Citation and Link Tiers

**Tier 1 -- entity-verification layer.** GBP, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook, plus the 10-20 directories that matter in your niche and country (UK trades: Checkatrade, Trustpilot, Yell, Gas Safe register, TrustATrader, local chamber). Perfect NAP. **Ten authoritative citations beat fifty junk ones**; mass submission is dead and mildly harmful.

**Tier 2 -- locally relevant editorial links** (the real ranking weight): sponsorships (clubs, charity runs, school fetes), local press, trade/industry bodies and manufacturer accredited-installer pages, non-competing local business partnerships, chamber of commerce where membership is real.

**Tier 3 -- linkable assets.** One genuinely useful local resource (water-hardness map, "new boiler cost in [region]" data page). Original data is the most reliably linkable format.

**Avoid:** bought links and guest-post networks, the 2026 flood of AI-generated link-sale sites, exact-match anchor campaigns, chasing DR/DA over relevance.
